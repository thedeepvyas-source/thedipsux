Flint-Link: A customer portal that answers before anyone has to email

02 · The problem

When a press was waiting on a certificate, the customer was waiting on an email

Placing an order was easy. Everything after it, from certificates and order updates to invoices and stock counts, meant emailing an account manager and waiting, sometimes while a press stood still.

Who

Printers and packaging converters who order, stock and pay for ink.

What went wrong

Every certificate, order update, invoice and stock count went through an account manager.

Why it mattered

A compliance document took 24 to 48 hours. On one site visit, production stopped 3 times in 2 hours while the team waited for a certificate.

The UX challenge

Let customers answer their own questions after they’ve placed an order, starting from the order they hold.

The loop

Needs a certificateOr an order update, or an invoice copy
Emails the account managerThen calls to chase it
Account manager searchesInternal systems and inboxes
Reply by emailA PDF or a status update

Every request took account-manager time away from other work.

04 · Design direction

Make the order the way in

The research all pointed the same way: customers think in terms of what they already have, an order or a delivery, not a document type. So the design starts there, keeps the five after-order jobs one step from the order, and shows the status of every request, so nobody has to email to ask.

The principle

Start from what the customer already holds: an order, a shipment, a batch.

05 · Decision 1 of 4 · Information architecture

Starting from the order, not a document library

The brief asked for a document library. But in Round 1, nobody could find the certificate for their last delivery in it. Customers knew their order, not the document type, so I rebuilt the structure around the order.

Options

The document library from the brief · a structure built around the order

Decision

Make the order the way in. Documents live on the shipment they came with.

Trade-off

Three sprints of rework, late in the project. The library stayed only for documents not tied to an order.

Result

Round 1: nobody found the certificate for their last delivery. Round 2: everyone did.

05 · Decision 2 of 4 · Consignment stock

Updating consignment stock on one screen

Consignment stock is ink that sits at the customer’s site until it’s used. Keeping Flint’s count up to date used to take a long email thread, so I put the whole job on one screen with a single submit.

Options

The email thread as it was · one screen with one submit

Decision

Both counts side by side, one figure per product, and one submit for everything.

Trade-off

A dense screen. The per-product status and the full history carry that weight.

Result

Not measured on its own.

05 · Decision 3 of 4 · Scope

Making the case for support, which wasn’t in the brief

In the brief, support stayed with account managers. But in testing, 4 of 12 participants brought it up first, without being asked. A problem on the press can’t wait for an email, so I wrote a business case for bringing support into the portal.

Options

Leave support with account managers, as briefed · chat answers plus tracked tickets

Decision

A Technical Support Agent for common problems, and a Support Hub for tracked tickets.

Trade-off

More scope, added late, and answers Flint’s team must keep current.

Result

Signed off in acceptance testing.

05 · Decision 4 of 4 · Downloads

Making large downloads look alive on slow networks

Before the build, the engineering lead pointed out something I’d missed: on slow factory networks, a large PDF looks stuck at zero. A download that looks stuck looks broken, so every download got the same three clear states.

Options

No visible state, as first designed · three explicit states

Decision

Downloading, Downloaded, and Failed with Try again, on every file.

Trade-off

Three more states to design and build for every download.

Result

After launch, the fix customers praised most.

On any certificate, data sheet or invoice

06 · Testing and iteration

What changed after testing, and what’s still open

The decisions above show what usability testing changed. Some things only showed up later, once the portal was in use. Here’s what we changed, and what still needs fixing.

DesignFindingDesign responseResult

One delivery to confirm per product

Real sites get several deliveries for one product.

Each delivery is confirmed on its own.

Reworked in a maintenance release.

What remained unresolved

IssueWhat’s wrongStatus
Status tag contrast

Every tag has a word, so color is never the only signal. But none reach 4.5:1: Not Submitted 2.4:1, Open 2.9:1, Closed 2.7:1, Submitted 3.0:1. The proposed colors reach 4.9:1 to 5.4:1.

Not fixed
Download button contrast

White on #25B67C is 2.6:1, while #17805A reaches 4.9:1. Icon-only buttons also need accessible names, and download states should be announced to screen readers.

Not fixed
Account approval wait

New accounts can wait 48 hours or more for approval. I designed a waiting state, but the wait itself wasn’t solved.

Not solved

08 · Reflection

Three things I took away from this project

The best idea in this portal came from a test that failed. These are the lessons from that, and from what I missed.

LessonThen, on this projectNow
01 · Start from what the customer holds

The library from the brief failed in Round 1, because customers think in orders, not document types.

I start every structure from what the customer already holds: an order, a shipment, a batch.

02 · Walk a real week on site visits

We assumed one delivery per product. Real sites get several, and a maintenance release had to fix it.

On site visits, I walk through a real week of deliveries, not a single order.

03 · Set the after-launch number on day one

Whether fewer requests reached account managers was never tracked.

I agree the after-launch number and its baseline at kickoff.

A future direction: the first two days

New accounts can wait 48 hours or more for approval. These concepts let customers complete their profile while they wait, and show how far approval has got, with a way to raise a request.

Concept designs, not shipped work.
