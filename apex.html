Apex Insights: Every facility’s numbers, and a nudge when one slips

A group of nursing homes kept its numbers in Power BI, Microsoft’s reporting tool. Apex Insights puts every report behind one front door, and warns the right leader when a facility’s number slips.

My roleLead UI/UX Designer
ClientA skilled nursing group
PlatformsDesktop web and Power BI
ScopeInformation architecture, portal screens, the report layout system, and client wireframe reviews
Deliverables131 portal screens and 8 Power BI report layouts

02 · The problem

The reports existed. Getting to the right one at the right moment didn’t.

The group already had Power BI reports for census (how many beds are filled), staffing, payroll and revenue. The data wasn’t missing. The problem was finding the right report in time, and noticing a slipping number before it turned into a cost.

Who

Executives, facility administrators and directors of nursing across a group of skilled nursing facilities.

What went wrong

Census, staffing, payroll and revenue lived in separate Power BI reports. To find a number, you had to know which report it was in.

Why it mattered

Staffing below target means overtime, agency cost, or a short shift. A number noticed three days late means three days of that cost.

The UX challenge

Give every report one front door, and bring problems to people before they go looking.

04 · Design direction

One home for every number, and alerts for the exceptions

By the end of the reviews, two needs were clear. Every report had to be easy to reach and read. And leaders shouldn’t have to go looking for problems. That gave me one principle, and it shaped the four decisions below.

The principle

Give every number one home, and send the exceptions to the people who own them.

05 · Decision 1 of 4 · Report actions

One place for every action

In my first wireframes, each report carried the portal’s buttons and Power BI’s own controls. Clone and Rename showed up twice, as different buttons in different places. I asked the client why, right on the frame, and pushed for one place for every action. The BI team wanted to keep Power BI’s own controls, since leaders already knew them. We met in the middle: Power BI’s icons stayed for pin and full screen.

In the final screens, every report has the same corner: pin and full screen as icons, and everything else in one menu, including export, print, subscribe, subscriptions for others, favorite, refresh, and, for people with permission, edit and delete.

Options

Keep both sets of actions · rebuild every action in the portal · one corner with Power BI’s actions inside it

Decision

Pin and full screen as icons, everything else in one menu, the same on every report.

Trade-off

Some Power BI options stay hidden from everyday users.

Result

Approved by the client. Not tested with users.

05 · Decision 2 of 4 · Layout

Let the reports have the width

My first home screen was a dense grid with a fixed sidebar. But Power BI tables need width, and that sidebar took 224px of it on every screen. In the March 29 round, I tried wider tiles with the sidebar as an overlay. The final home keeps that idea: an icon rail that expands only when you need it.

Options

Keep the fixed sidebar · move navigation to a top bar · a rail that collapses to icons

Decision

A collapsed icon rail and wider tiles, so Power BI tables get the width.

Trade-off

Navigation is one move away, not always in view.

Result

Approved by the client. Not tested with users.

05 · Decision 3 of 4 · Alerts and subscriptions

Bring the numbers to people

My “What is alerts?” question had a simple answer in the end: leaders don’t want to open a portal just to check whether something’s wrong. So the portal goes to them. Subscriptions send a report on a schedule and can be paused. File storage keeps every run with its status. Alerts go out when a facility crosses a line.

The routine goes on a schedule. Only the exceptions interrupt anyone.

Options

Email every report on a schedule · alert on every change · schedules for the routine, thresholds for exceptions

Decision

Subscriptions for the routine, and three alert levels tied to a line per facility.

Trade-off

Someone at the client has to own each threshold, or alerts become noise.

Result

Approved by the client. Same-day action on alerts isn’t measured yet.

05 · Decision 4 of 4 · Report layout

One layout for every Power BI report

Different people had built the Power BI reports, each with their own header and filters. I designed one layout for all of them, so once you’ve learned one report, you can read the rest. Same header, same filters, same chips, and drill-throughs (clicking a number to see its detail) that always have a way back.

Options

Each builder lays out their own · one fixed template · one shared frame with free content

Decision

One shared frame: refresh, tabs, filter chips, headline numbers and expandable rows, always in the same places.

Trade-off

Report builders give up some freedom.

Result

08 · Reflection

Three things I took away from this project

The most useful thing I did on this project wasn’t a screen. It was asking questions early. These are the habits I’ve kept.

LessonThen, on this projectNow
01 · Questions on the frames, first

Alerts and file storage were just names in a brief until I asked what they were for.

My first review is always questions, written next to the screens they’re about.

02 · Check the copy before sign-off

Lorem ipsum and typos like “Exceutive” and “Unable to Complie” reached approved screens.

I run a copy check before anything goes for sign-off.

03 · Agree the number at sign-off

The screens were approved with no way to see whether they worked.

The number to track is agreed in the same meeting as sign-off.

A future direction: one sign-in for both portals

The client also runs a second portal for day-to-day management. The next design joins the two behind one sign-in, so a leader can go from a number to an action without logging in twice. The weekly staffing module, with drill-throughs for HPPD, overtime, agency and spend, is already designed in the same layout.
