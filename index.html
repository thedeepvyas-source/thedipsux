Five recent case studies

01

IGEL Licence Portal

Licence portal · Desktop web · RFP response

A portal for admins who
open it twice a year

As design lead on Apexon’s response, I redesigned the portal around what admins need to do next: a Home that flags what to renew, licence states in plain language, and a Copilot for partners. IGEL clicked through the prototype, and we won the engagement.

IGEL is going ahead with the redesign, built from our prototype.

02

myAster

Patient app · iOS and Android

Telling patients what their insurance allows before they book, not after

Insured patients only found out at checkout that they couldn’t book. As lead designer in a team of four, I designed the parts every service shares first, from patient profiles and insurance states to one checkout, so each rule is checked while the patient can still act on it.

Patients book appointments, manage family profiles, and access care through one app.

View case study →
03

PetTrackr

Mobile app · iOS and Android

Making daily pet diabetes tracking easier

Owners test their pet’s glucose at 7 a.m. while holding a restless animal. As the only designer, I ran in-home interviews and a 14-day diary study, then rebuilt onboarding after the first usability round showed setup stopping people before their first reading.

Pet owners can now log readings, track glucose trends, and share reports with their vet.

View case study →
04

Flint-Link

Customer portal · Desktop and mobile web

A customer portal that answers before anyone has to email

The brief asked for a document library, but in the first usability round people couldn’t find their most recent certificate in it. Working from 14 customer interviews and two site visits, I rebuilt the information architecture around the order and designed responsive workflows on top of it.

Customers can now access documents, check orders, and manage consignment stock without emailing an account manager.

View case study →
05

Apex Insights

Reporting portal · Desktop web and Power BI

Every facility’s numbers, and a nudge when one slips

The brief named features like Alerts and File storage without saying what they were for. I put my questions on the first wireframes, used the client’s answers to set the scope, and gave every report the same actions and layout.

Teams access reports, monitor staffing alerts, and schedule updates from one portal.

How I lead and design

Understanding people

I use research to understand what people need, what gets in their way, and which problems deserve attention.

Setting a direction

I turn those insights into clear priorities, weighing user needs, business goals, and technical constraints with the team.

Shaping the experience

I design flows, interfaces, and interactions, using prototypes and feedback to refine how the experience works and feels.

Leading through delivery

I guide designers, work closely with stakeholders and engineers, and stay involved through reviews, handoff, and implementation.

03About

An engineer by training,
working in UI/⁠UX since 2014.

I’m Deep, and I’m based in Ahmedabad, India. I studied electronics and communications engineering, started out designing graphics and interfaces, and have worked in UI/⁠UX ever since, mostly on client projects for companies in very different fields.

Changing domains every few projects taught me to ask good questions early and to lean on the people who know the domain best: users, client teams and engineers. I’d rather we get to the right answer together than have me guess it alone.

Outside client work, I run @the.deep.ux, a UX community of more than 20,000 people.
